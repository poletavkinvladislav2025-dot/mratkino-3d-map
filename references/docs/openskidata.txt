What's in the data
Four datasets. They are available on their own
            or together in a single GeoPackage, and every feature carries its
            source, the ski area it belongs to, and the country, region and
            locality it sits in.
Ski areas
Resorts and nordic centres, clustered from OpenStreetMap
site=piste
relations and
landuse=winter_sports
areas, then merged with
                Skimap.org records. Points, polygons or multipolygons.
Downhill and nordic activity flags
Operating status and run colour convention
Official websites and Wikidata ID
Computed statistics: run length and count by difficulty, lift
                  count by type, elevation range and vertical
Runs
Every OpenStreetMap piste â downhill, nordic, ski touring,
                sledding, winter hiking and more. Note that a single trail as
                signed on the mountain is often several run features, split
                where it branches or where its tags change.
Difficulty, colour and regional difficulty convention
Grooming, lit, gladed, patrolled, oneway, snowmaking
Elevation profile, ascent and descent, average and max pitch
Uses: downhill, nordic, skitour, sled, hike, snow park…
Lifts
Aerialways and winter-sports railways: gondolas, chairlifts,
                drag lifts, funiculars and more. Railways other than funiculars
                are included only when they belong to a
site=piste
relation.
Lift type, status and access restriction
Capacity, occupancy, duration and ride speed
Detachable, bubble, heating
Inclined length, vertical and overall pitch
Spots
Point features in and around ski areas: lift stations, places
                where a run crosses a road or path, avalanche transceiver
                training areas and checkpoints, and halfpipes.
Lift stations with position, boarding and alighting
Crossings with dismount requirement
Avalanche transceiver training and checkpoints
Halfpipes
Feature IDs are content hashes.
They change
            whenever the feature changes, so they are not stable between daily

            OpenStreetMap or Skimap.org source ID, both of which are included.

How the data is made
Every day, the
openskidata-processor
pipeline rebuilds the whole dataset from scratch.
Extract
Pull winter sports features out of OpenStreetMap â
piste:type
ways and areas,
aerialway
lines,
landuse=winter_sports
areas and
site=piste
relations â alongside the ski area
                records from Skimap.org.
Normalize
Clean up tagging inconsistencies, merge overlapping ways into a
                single run with multiple uses, and join connected segments that
                share the same properties.
Cluster
Group runs and lifts into ski areas using piste relations,
                winter sports landuse and proximity, then merge the
                OpenStreetMap and Skimap.org views of the same resort into one
                feature.
Augment
Add elevation to every coordinate, reverse-geocode each feature
                to a country, region and locality, compute per-ski-area
                statistics from the runs and lifts that belong to it.
Export
Write out GeoJSON, CSV and GeoPackage â plus the vector tiles
                that power OpenSkiMap.org.
Improve the data
OpenSkiData has no separate database to edit. Everything except the
            Skimap.org ski area records comes straight from OpenStreetMap, so
            the way to fix or add something is to edit OpenStreetMap â your
            change flows into the dataset within a few days.
Tagging a ski area
Ski areas are the trickiest part of the pipeline, and good
                tagging is what keeps a resort from being split into duplicates:
Start with a
landuse=winter_sports
area. It's
                  easy to add and easy to maintain.
If that isn't explicit enough, add a
site=piste
relation as well â but make sure it
                  contains
all
of the resort's lifts and runs. If many
                  are missing, the merge will fail.
Where one resort needs several separate areas, combine them
                  into a single multipolygon relation rather than leaving them
                  as separate winter sports areas.
